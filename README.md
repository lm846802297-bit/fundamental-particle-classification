# Fundamental Particle Classification Beyond Bose and Fermi

A research project on the possible kinds of fundamental particles and statistics in relativistic quantum field theory (QFT), across spacetime dimensions.

## Central question

Is the familiar boson/fermion dichotomy exhaustive for fundamental particles, or does it follow only under specific assumptions about spacetime dimension, localization, locality, and the structure of the theory?

The project will compare established results before proposing new categories or research gaps.

## Scope

- The primary objects are physical one-particle states and irreducible superselection sectors in relativistic QFT. The project will state explicitly which meaning is used in each argument; these notions are related but are not assumed to be identical.
- The analysis is not restricted to 3+1 dimensions. Lower- and higher-dimensional spacetime theories are in scope when they clarify the classification.
- Candidate structures include Bose and Fermi statistics, parastatistics, braid-group statistics (including abelian anyons and non-abelian plektons), and other consistent possibilities found in the literature.
- The project distinguishes particle statistics from spacetime-symmetry representations, superselection sectors, fusion rules, and particle species.
- Condensed-matter quasiparticles, collective excitations, and emergent particles are outside the research scope.

## Questions to answer

1. What assumptions make the Bose/Fermi classification theorem apply?
2. How do dimension, localization, and locality change the allowed exchange or statistics structures?
3. Which proposed categories describe distinct physical sectors, and which can be reformulated using internal or gauge structure?
4. How do spin-statistics relations, spacetime-group representations, and superselection theory fit together?
5. Which gaps remain after a source-based review, and what result would be genuinely new?

## Research workflow

1. Map primary literature and record the hypotheses of each theorem.
2. Build a dimension-by-dimension comparison of particle notions, localization, statistics, symmetry representations, and fusion.
3. Check candidate examples and counterexamples against the stated assumptions.
4. Identify research gaps only after comparing the primary sources and existing reviews.

See [the research program](docs/research-program.md) and [the literature review template](docs/literature-review-template.md).

## Working standards

- Separate proved statements, model-dependent results, interpretations, and open conjectures.
- Cite primary sources and record stable identifiers such as DOI or arXiv ID.
- State theorem assumptions and definitions before comparing conclusions.
- Keep calculations, inputs, environment details, and native outputs with their source version when computational work is added.
- Develop substantive changes on a branch and submit them for pull-request review.

## Status

This repository establishes the research scope and workflow. It does not yet claim an exhaustive classification or a new theorem.
