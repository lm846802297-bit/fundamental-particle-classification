# 1+1D locality barrier: the massive DHR criterion and Haag duality

**Result.** In a Haag-dual observable net with the split property for wedges, Müger's theorem excludes nontrivial sectors that look like the **same** vacuum on every left and right wedge. The theorem does not exclude ordinary massive particles in the vacuum representation. Dropping double-cone Haag duality alone makes a precise difference: the parity-even fixed-point net of the massive real scalar has wedge split and an inequivalent, bounded-region-localizable DHR sector with an isolated mass shell. An explicit globally even bilocal operator witnesses the loss of duality. That sector's particle is still an ordinary boson.

**中文摘要：** 双锥 Haag 对偶性与楔区分裂性质同时成立时，看起来与同一真空在任一左、右楔区都等价的扇区必然平凡。只去掉双锥对偶性，结论即可失效：有质量自由标量的偶宇称可观测网仍有楔区分裂性质，但奇宇称扇区有孤立质量壳和 DHR 局域性。可用一个双局域算符直接验证对偶性失效。这不是新统计的例子。

## 1. The theorem and its mass-shell scope

In [Müger, arXiv:hep-th/9705019v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9705019), use Definition 2.4, Proposition 2.5 and Theorem 3.1. For a vacuum representation $\pi_0$ of a local $1+1$D observable net, write $R(W)=\pi_0(A(W))''$. The hypotheses at issue are:

- **Haag duality for double cones:** $\pi_0(A(O))'=\pi_0(A(O'))''$, where the causal complement $O'$ contains the left and right complementary wedges, and $A(O')$ is generated by the bounded local algebras contained in it.
- **Split property for wedges (SPW):** for strictly separated wedge pairs $W_1\Subset W_2'$ the multiplication map extends to a spatial normal isomorphism $R(W_1)\bar\otimes R(W_2)\cong R(W_1)\vee R(W_2)$, in the standard vacuum setting of the theorem. Mere split for bounded double cones is not this assumption. Wedge duality follows under the two hypotheses but cannot be substituted for double-cone Haag duality in the theorem.

If $\pi|_{A(W)}\cong\pi_0|_{A(W)}$ for every wedge of **both** handednesses, Theorem 3.1 gives a countable vacuum multiple, and irreducible $\pi$ is the vacuum representation. DHR condition (1.1) is stronger: any wedge fits inside the causal complement of a suitable distant double cone, so equivalence on every such complement restricts to equivalence on every wedge. The theorem uses no mass-shell hypothesis in this implication. The particle question imposes an additional, separate joint translation spectrum condition: the lower spectral boundary in a candidate charged representation is the positive mass shell $H_m^+$, $m>0$, isolated from higher invariant masses. A candidate with that spectrum **and** the DHR condition cannot be a nontrivial sector of the stated Haag-dual wedge-split net. This is neither a spectrum theorem for the vacuum representation nor a theorem about all $1+1$D relativistic models or chiral sectors.

## 2. Exact compatibility example when double-cone duality is dropped

Let $\mathcal F(O)$ be the local real free scalar field net of mass $m>0$ on the symmetric Fock space $\mathcal H$. It has a vacuum $\Omega$ and the unbroken parity $G=(-1)^N$, with eigenspaces $\mathcal H_\pm$. Field-net Haag duality and the free-field example are discussed in Müger §7(a). [Buchholz and Lechner, §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0402072) prove the free massive field's wedge split via modular nuclearity. Here we consider a **different** net: the parity-fixed observables $A(O)=\mathcal F(O)^{\operatorname{Ad}G}$ with vacuum representation $\pi_+=A|_{\mathcal H_+}$. Its charged representation is $\pi_-=A|_{\mathcal H_-}$. This is the fixed-net setup of Müger §6.1, Eqs. (6.2)–(6.4). Since averaging satisfies $E(B)\Omega=P_+B\Omega$, cyclicity of $\Omega$ for field double cones implies cyclicity for observable double cones on $\mathcal H_+$.

**Sector and mass shell.** The global field algebra has weak closure $B(\mathcal H)$. Averaging over $1,\operatorname{Ad}G$ is normal, so the weak closure of the global invariant algebra on $\mathcal H$ is $B(\mathcal H_+)\oplus B(\mathcal H_-)$. Its commutant has no nonzero off-diagonal intertwiners, proving irreducibility of each restriction and their inequivalence. Positive-energy translations preserve parity. In $\mathcal H_-$ the $n=1$ subspace is the mass-$m$ scalar shell; all other odd $n$ satisfy $n\geq3$ and
$$
\left(\sum_{j=1}^n p_j\right)^2
=m^2\left[n+2\sum_{i<j}\cosh(\theta_i-\theta_j)\right]\geq n^2m^2 .
$$
Here $p_j=m(\cosh\theta_j,\sinh\theta_j)$. The odd-sector continuum starts at $3m$ as an infimum. The one-particle state is the elementary continuum scalar, not a composite or medium excitation.

**DHR implementation.** For every double cone $O$ choose a real test function $f$ supported there with nonzero one-particle smearing, and set $u_O=\operatorname{sgn}\phi(f)$. The smeared field acts on its one-mode factor as a nonzero multiple of the oscillator position, with no atom at zero, so $u_O$ is a bounded self-adjoint unitary. Its spectral functional calculus is in $\mathcal F(O)$ (the local Weyl operators of the smeared field generate it), and $Gu_OG=-u_O$. Thus $u_O$ maps $\mathcal H_+\leftrightarrow\mathcal H_-$. Locality makes it commute with $A(O')$, intertwining the even and odd representations on this whole complement. The DHR condition holds for all $O$ even though $\pi_-$ is not globally vacuum-equivalent. The grading is internal parity, not fermion parity: the full scalar field is local and the charged operators $u_{O_1},u_{O_2}$ commute for spacelike separated $O_1,O_2$.

## 3. Split survives the fixed-point restriction; duality does not

**Inheritance of SPW.** For any split pair let $F_i=\mathcal F(W_i)$. In the field-net split isomorphism $F_1\bar\otimes F_2\cong F_1\vee F_2$, global parity acts as the simultaneous parity. The subnet generated by the **separately** invariant wedge algebras corresponds to $F_1^G\bar\otimes F_2^G$ under the same multiplication map. Here $F_i^G$ is the weak closure of its bounded-region fixed subalgebras: approximate in the increasing w*-dense field union and use normal averaging. Restricting $F_i^G$ and their join to $\mathcal H_+$ gives faithful normal representations. Indeed $\Omega\in\mathcal H_+$ is separating for the field join of the two wedges, because it is cyclic for a field double cone spacelike to both. An operator vanishing on $\mathcal H_+$ annihilates $\Omega$ and is zero in that join. The already-normal product isomorphism hence descends through faithful restriction and implements precisely the split map for observable wedge algebras in $\pi_+$. This argument uses more than the inclusion of the subnet in a split field net.

**Explicit failure of double-cone Haag duality.** Let $W_L,W_R$ be the two components of $O'$. Choose unitary odd operators $u_L,u_R$ by the preceding construction inside bounded regions of these wedges. Their product $B=u_Lu_R$ is invariant under **simultaneous** parity and commutes with $A(O)$ by locality. Its restriction $B_+$ therefore belongs to $\pi_+(A(O))'$. Under the split isomorphism the automorphism applying parity only on the left factor fixes $F_L^G\vee F_R^G$ pointwise, but sends $B$ to $-B$. Since $B$ is nonzero it does not belong to that invariant join. If $B_+$ belonged to $\pi_+(A(O'))''$, faithfulness of restriction on the globally invariant field join would lift that membership back to $F_L^G\vee F_R^G$, a contradiction. Thus
$$
\pi_+(A(O'))''\ \varsubsetneqq\ \pi_+(A(O))'.
$$
Only the fixed observable net in its even vacuum representation enters this inequality, not the full field net. The source also notes that this fixed net retains wedge duality (§6.1). A dual completion is a change of net; Müger Proposition 6.1 forbids a nontrivial DHR sector of this type from extending to a sector that remains DHR- or wedge-localized in the dual net.

## 4. Nonidentical asymptotic vacua are a different problem

In Müger §5, Eq. (5.1), a soliton representation has possibly different reference vacua for all left and all right wedges. If both references are the same vacuum and Haag duality plus SPW hold, Theorem 3.1 applies. If the references differ, the hypothesis of Theorem 3.1 is not supplied merely by (5.1). Under the separate hypotheses of Proposition 5.1 and Theorem 5.2 (irreducible solitons, with at least one asymptotic vacuum Haag-dual and wedge-split), the associated wedge morphism has index one and the soliton type, if realized, is unique up to equivalence for a given pair of asymptotic vacua. Those theorems concern representations and localization. They neither construct an isolated mass shell for every permitted pair nor furnish a nontrivial exchange braid for a massive kink.

**Bottom line:** a massive shell and the DHR criterion do not force the two net assumptions. The parity-fixed net shows exactly which assumption fails while the other holds. Neither this counterexample nor the no-go theorem settles statistics outside the stated localization and net assumptions.
