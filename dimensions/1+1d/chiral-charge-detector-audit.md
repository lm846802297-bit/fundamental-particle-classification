# 1+1D: an observable detector resolves the ray, not the number of chiral charges

**Result.** A nonzero finite-energy wave in the [chiral charged sector](chiral-current-wave-statistics-audit.md) has a nonzero positive asymptotic detector response concentrated at the right-going velocity. An ordered two-detector response can also be strictly positive on that same wave, but its velocity measure is again concentrated on the diagonal, and separated velocity acceptances give zero. These observables do not turn the sector braid into an exchange of two identified asymptotic particles.

## Setup and one-detector response

Use the product current net in the representation $(q,0)$, $q=1/\sqrt{2}$, with right coordinate $u=t-x$ and left coordinate $v=t+x$. Put $\Psi=\psi\otimes\Omega_{\rm L}\ne0$ in the right null-wave subspace and require $\Psi=P_E\Psi$ for some finite energy cutoff $E$. Let $\beta_s$ be translation of the right chiral factor, generated by the positive operator $K_q$ in this representation. The chiral representation is irreducible and $\ker K_q=0$ as shown in the earlier audit.

Choose a *neutral observable* $B=A(f)\otimes1$, where $A$ is a bounded local right-current observable and $A(f)=\int ds\,f(s)\beta_s(A)$; take $f$ Schwartz with $\operatorname{supp}\widehat f$ a compact subset of $(-\infty,0)$ in the convention $\widehat f(k)\propto\int ds\,e^{iks}f(s)$. The time-energy transfer is negative and bounded away from zero. Truncating the smearing to a bounded interval gives an approximant local in a larger double cone, with a norm error decreasing faster than any inverse power of its size; the left spectator is the identity. Thus $B$ is almost local and energy decreasing and $B^*B$ is an admissible detector. [Dybalski–Tanimoto, arXiv:1101.5700v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.5700v3), Lemma A.1(b), ensures that such an $A,f$ can be chosen with $A(f)\psi\ne0$, since otherwise $\psi$ would be translation invariant. Their Theorem 2.9 bounds the spatial integral uniformly on energy-bounded states. Those are the existence and finiteness inputs; no vacuum scattering theorem is applied to the charged sector.

For nonnegative bounded continuous $g$ and $t>0$, define the response with velocity acceptance $g$:
$$
D_{B,t}(g)=\int_{\mathbb R}dx\,g(x/t)\|\alpha_{(t,x)}(B)\Psi\|^2
 =\int_{\mathbb R}du\,g(1-u/t)\|\beta_u(A(f))\psi\|^2 .
$$
In particular $D_{B,t}(1)=C_B$ is exactly independent of $t$, with $0<C_B<\infty$ by continuity at $u=0$ and the cited bound. Dominated convergence gives
$$
D_{B,t}(g)\longrightarrow C_B\,g(1).
$$
The same holds after any nonnegative time averaging of integral one supported at $t=T+o(T)$; the integrals already converge as $t\to+\infty$. This gives a velocity measure $C_B\delta_{+1}$ and strengthens the unweighted positive signal of [Dybalski–Tanimoto, Eq. (3.22)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.5700v3). Proposition 2.10 there relates nonzero asymptotic detector functionals to particle weights; the ray measure here is a particular response, not a computation of the full weight or its irreducible decomposition.

## Two-detector response: positivity, direction, and energy threshold

For operators $B_1,B_2$ of the above form, write $b_i=A_i(f_i)$. Consider the explicitly ordered positive sequential response
$$
D_{21,t}(g_1,g_2)=\int dx_1\,dx_2\,g_1(x_1/t)g_2(x_2/t)
 \|\alpha_{(t,x_2)}(B_2)\alpha_{(t,x_1)}(B_1)\Psi\|^2 .
$$
This uses the observable quadratic form $(B_2B_1)^*(B_2B_1)$ with the indicated independent translates. It is not an identification with a symmetrized product of counters or an assumed two-particle number operator. The absolute integral is finite: $B_1(t,x_1)\Psi$ remains in $P_E\mathcal H$ by negative energy transfer, so the Theorem 2.9 bound in $x_2$ is at most $c_{2,E}\|B_1(t,x_1)\Psi\|^2$; another application in $x_1$ finishes. Changing variables separately and applying dominated convergence yields
$$
D_{21,t}(g_1,g_2)\longrightarrow C_{21}\,g_1(1)g_2(1),
\qquad
C_{21}=\int du_1\,du_2\,\|\beta_{u_2}(b_2)\beta_{u_1}(b_1)\psi\|^2 .
$$
The same limit holds for normalized time averaging supported at $T+o(T)$. If the velocity acceptances have disjoint supports, the limit is zero. Yet for each nonzero bounded-energy $\psi$ one can choose $b_1\psi\ne0$ and then choose $b_2b_1\psi\ne0$ by applying the lemma again to $b_1\psi$, a nonzero vector of the same irreducible charged representation. Strong continuity makes a neighborhood of $(u_1,u_2)=(0,0)$ contribute, proving strict positivity for these choices. The sequential response concerns two actions on an input wave in one fixed charged representation; neither its sign nor its multiplicity assigns an underlying elementary-particle count.

The independent spectral check is exact at any finite time: if the support of each $\widehat f_i$ is contained in $(-\infty,-\delta_i]$, $\delta_i>0$, the product sends $P_E\mathcal H$ into $P_{E-\delta_1-\delta_2}\mathcal H$, taken as zero for a negative cutoff. In particular $E\leq\delta_1+\delta_2$ forces zero response regardless of the ray acceptance: the equality case uses the absence of a zero-energy vector in the charged representation. This uses energy transfer rather than an assumed wave count; the detector class also does not convert a transported charge's numerical value into a count of constituent charges.

Finally the two ordered charged preparations $FG\Omega$ and $GF\Omega$ in the [co-moving audit](chiral-comoving-exchange-audit.md) differ by one global phase. Their state restrictions on the bounded observable algebra coincide. Every energy-filtered detector response of the above type consequently agrees for the two preparations, for each cutoff and each time, whenever defined. This is not a ban on phase-sensitive interference using additional coherently controlled reference preparations. It establishes that these detector responses, applied to the two separate ordered products, cannot read their relative braid phase.
