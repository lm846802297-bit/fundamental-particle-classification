# 1+1D checkpoint: a covariant braid is not enough to identify particle statistics

**Phase conclusion.** In the standard-Minkowski cases audited so far, nontrivial sector braiding, nontrivial collision phases, sharp particle or wave spectra, and positive detector responses are all established in particular examples. None of the cited constructions identifies a non-Bose/Fermi exchange of two independently prepared identical fundamental asymptotic particles in the same theory. This is a conclusion about the evidence below, not a $1+1$D no-go theorem. The deformed-spacetime candidate is outside this checkpoint.

## New kinematic check: covariance allows every phase

Take just one copy of the ordinary positive right-null momentum orbit, with Hilbert space $\mathcal K=L^2(\mathbb R_+,dp/p)$, so $P^0=P^1=p$. A translation acts by multiplication by $e^{ip(t-x)}$; a boost rescales $p$ by a common positive factor in the wavefunction argument, preserving $dp/p$. This is a kinematic comparison input. It is not an identification of the entire charged wave subspace with one irreducible particle species.

On the two-input space $\mathcal K\otimes\mathcal K$, change variables to total energy $R=p_1+p_2>0$ and momentum fraction $r=p_1/R\in(0,1)$. The change of variables gives
$$
dp_1\,dp_2=R\,dR\,dr,\qquad
\frac{dp_1}{p_1}\frac{dp_2}{p_2}=\frac{dR}{R}\frac{dr}{r(1-r)} .
$$
Translations act only by $e^{iR(t-x)}$, and boosts rescale $R$ while leaving $r$ fixed. Thus any unitary on the fraction factor commutes with the diagonal connected Poincaré action. In particular the slot flip $F$ acts by $r\mapsto1-r$, preserving the fraction measure, and each $E_\theta=e^{i\theta}F$ is equally covariant. The squares of $F$, $-F$, and $iF$ are respectively $1$, $1$, and $-1$; this last sign cannot be changed by a unitary conjugation of the single operator. On the unsymmetrized $n$-input space, replacing each adjacent flip $F_j$ by $e^{i\theta}F_j$ satisfies every Artin braid relation: each nearest-neighbor relation contains three phases on both sides and each distant commutation two. Thus even covariance plus braid relations on this test space does not fix whether exchange squares to $1$. With phase $i$ these operators represent the Artin braid group, not the symmetric group; $iF$ has no $+1$ eigenspace. Its square is invariant under conjugating the fixed operator but not under rephasing the generator itself. The phase was chosen by hand, with no observable net, localization, symmetrization prescription, or scattering construction selecting it.

## What would upgrade the chiral candidate

Construct two-charge maps from independently specified charged wave packets (or justify a definite fusion input space) into the physical $(2q,0)$ representation of the **same** net, constructing, rather than assuming, a scattering subspace. Specify the charged interpolation operators, the two localization or transport arrangements, the convergence and inner products, and how their output depends on changing field representatives. A sufficient result would be isometric maps onto a common physical range, with a controlled comparison for the two orientations whose closed comparison intertwines the net's monodromy on that range. Isometry and Poincaré covariance alone are not sufficient by the calculation above: the maps must be derived rather than assigned, and an arbitrary separate rephasing of unrelated preparation maps is not evidence. A normalized operator identity or a comparison against a common coherent reference would supply more evidence than a phase between separately normalized output rays.

For example, if the net supplies two unit-norm preparations $W_+\phi$ and $W_-\phi$ in the same charge representation and a single controlled preparation relative to one reference, the normalized state $2^{-1/2}(|0\rangle W_+\phi+|1\rangle W_-\phi)$ has reference Pauli $X$ and $Y$ means equal to the real and imaginary parts of $\langle W_+\phi,W_-\phi\rangle$. For a pair of coherent histories differing by the closed phase $-1$ rather than $+1$, the $X$ means are respectively $-1$ and $+1$, although either individual preparation gives the same wave ray. This is a conditional discriminator, not a claim that such controlled preparation or histories already exist in the current net.

The existing co-moving calculation fails that upgrade by a particular mechanism: it never compares the two arrangements dynamically. The detector result fails it for a different reason: these responses do not identify two independently incoming charged excitations and are insensitive to global phase. This leaves the $1+1$D phase open at a specific construction, without extrapolating a local negative result to all possible definitions of asymptotic exchange.
