# Research Program

## 1. Objective: establish existence or prove impossibility

The central objective is to determine whether **there exists a fundamental particle in relativistic quantum field theory (QFT) that is genuinely outside both the ordinary Bose and Fermi classes**.

This is an existence problem. The project is not limited to parastatistics and is not merely an exercise in compiling particle classifications. It searches across candidate statistics, spacetime dimensions, particle representations, localization structures, and superselection sectors for a physically consistent counterexample to the usual Bose-Fermi dichotomy.

The decisive outcomes are:

1. **Existence:** construct or identify an explicit relativistic QFT with a physical fundamental particle whose exchange/statistics or particle-sector structure is operationally and mathematically inequivalent to ordinary bosons and fermions, including theories that merely add internal or gauge labels.
2. **No-go:** prove that no such particle can exist under a precisely stated set of assumptions, and identify which assumptions are essential.

A result that only identifies a new species, unusual mass or charge, higher spin, continuous spin, helicity, or a different field notation does not by itself establish a particle beyond the Bose-Fermi classes. Such representations remain important to the search because they may constrain candidate constructions and the applicable spin-statistics theorems.

## 2. Definitions and particle criteria

For every source and argument, specify which object is being discussed:

- **Physical one-particle state:** a state in the physical Hilbert space with a particle interpretation. In theories where the standard criterion applies, this may be an isolated positive-energy mass-shell representation. If that criterion fails or is unavailable, state the alternative particle criterion explicitly.
- **Asymptotic particle:** a state that enters the incoming/outgoing scattering-state construction, when scattering theory is available.
- **Superselection sector:** a representation of the observable theory that cannot be connected to another by local observable operations. Record the localization, transportability, and spectrum assumptions used.
- **Field and charge description:** a choice of fields and internal or gauge degrees of freedom used to represent physical sectors. A field label or a gauge-dependent excitation is not automatically a distinct physical particle.

These notions are related but not interchangeable. A proposed candidate must specify which physical object carries the claimed non-Bose/non-Fermi property and how it is detected in the observable theory.

## 3. Candidate and equivalence tests

For every proposed candidate, answer the following:

1. **Fundamental status:** Is it a particle of the underlying relativistic QFT, rather than a composite bound state, medium-dependent quasiparticle, collective excitation, or emergent excitation?
2. **Physical state:** What is its physical one-particle state or particle-bearing superselection sector?
3. **Non-Bose/Fermi property:** Which exchange algebra, braid/monodromy representation, permutation-group representation, or other invariant distinguishes it from ordinary Bose and Fermi statistics?
4. **Observable distinction:** Can the distinction survive passage to the physical observable algebra and physical Hilbert space?
5. **Inequivalence:** Can the candidate be rewritten as ordinary bosons or fermions with additional internal/gauge degrees of freedom without changing physical predictions? If yes, it is not a new statistics class for this project.
6. **Consistency:** Does the construction satisfy the stated spectrum, positivity, covariance, locality/causality, and interaction requirements?

A mathematical exchange algebra alone is insufficient. It must define a physically admissible particle in a relativistic QFT.

## 4. Comparison axes

For every theorem, construction, or proposed particle type, record:

| Axis | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Spacetime | Which dimension, metric, and spacetime symmetry group are assumed? |
| Particle criterion | Is the object a physical one-particle state, an asymptotic particle, a particle-bearing sector, or something else? |
| Poincare representation | Which massive or massless irreducible representations and little groups occur? |
| Localization | Are fields or charges point-localized, bounded-region localized, string-localized, cone-localized, or localized by another criterion? |
| Causality | Which locality or commutativity conditions apply to observables and fields? |
| Statistics | Is the exchange structure permutation-based, braid-based, or more general? Which representations or categories occur? |
| Composition | What fusion rules, antiparticles, sector products, and multiparticle states are allowed? |
| Spin and symmetry | Which spacetime-group representations occur, and what spin-statistics relation is established? |
| Physical distinction | Which observable or invariant distinguishes the candidate from Bose/Fermi particles? |
| Equivalence | Can the description be reformulated through ordinary internal or gauge structure without changing physical sectors or predictions? |
| Consistency | Are positivity, unitarity, spectrum, covariance, locality/causality, and CPT assumptions satisfied? |
| Evidence | Is the claim a theorem, explicit construction, model-dependent result, conjecture, or review-level summary? |

## 5. Work packages

### WP0 — Primary-source map

Review primary literature and reliable reviews on:

- Wigner's classification and irreducible representations of the Poincare group, including massive and massless little groups across spacetime dimensions;
- higher-spin and continuous-spin representations, while distinguishing new spacetime representations from new statistics;
- spin-statistics theorems, their hypotheses, and dimension dependence;
- parastatistics and its relation to internal, gauge, and superselection structures;
- braid-group statistics, abelian anyons, non-abelian plektons, and relativistic QFT in 2+1 dimensions;
- infinite statistics and other generalized exchange or field algebras;
- superselection sectors, DHR theory, Doplicher-Roberts reconstruction, and related extensions;
- point-, string-, and cone-localized fields, observable locality, and causal localization;
- spectrum conditions, positivity, unitarity, CPT, and interaction constraints relevant to candidate theories.

For each source, record the exact theorem or construction, definitions, hypotheses, proof strategy, physical state space, and known limitations. A gap in a review is a lead to investigate, not evidence of novelty.

### WP1 — Dimension-aware candidate matrix

Build a matrix for 1+1, 2+1, 3+1, and higher-dimensional spacetime. Keep separate columns for:

1. physical particle criterion;
2. Poincare representations;
3. exchange/statistics structures;
4. localization and causality;
5. superselection sectors and fusion;
6. physical inequivalence to ordinary Bose/Fermi descriptions;
7. consistency status and primary-source evidence.

Do not infer that a candidate exists merely because a mathematical representation or exchange algebra can be written down.

### WP2 — Candidate verification

For each candidate type, identify an explicit relativistic QFT construction or a precise obstruction. Verify that:

- the particle is fundamental under the project's definition;
- the physical Hilbert space and observable sector are well defined;
- the candidate's non-Bose/Fermi property is not a gauge or internal-label reformulation;
- the construction meets its claimed dimension, spectrum, locality, and unitarity assumptions;
- interactions, antiparticles, and multiparticle composition are treated consistently where required.

### WP3 — Existence proof or no-go assessment

Compare the verified candidates with the relevant primary theorems. For each surviving candidate, specify the exact construction and its assumptions. For each obstruction, specify the theorem's hypotheses and whether relaxing any one of them admits a genuine candidate.

A candidate research gap must state:

- what is already known;
- the exact unanswered existence question;
- why existing theorems or constructions do not settle it;
- what mathematical or physical result would close the gap;
- what would distinguish that result from a reformulation or change of terminology.

### WP4 — Focused result

Select a tractable question only after WP0–WP3. Develop the construction or proof, verify its assumptions, and submit the result with source history and independent checks.

## 6. Initial literature-phase acceptance criteria

The initial literature phase is complete when:

- the principal existence question and the operational meaning of "beyond Bose and Fermi" are explicit;
- each major classification claim is tied to a primary source;
- theorem assumptions are recorded in a comparable form;
- the matrix distinguishes particle states, asymptotic particles, sectors, and field descriptions;
- candidate statistics are tested for physical inequivalence to ordinary Bose/Fermi theories;
- dimension, Poincare representation, localization, and consistency conditions are represented;
- candidate gaps are checked against the cited results;
- unresolved questions are separated from established conclusions.

These criteria close the literature-baseline phase. Further model construction or no-go work belongs to the next phase and does not block completion of the baseline.
