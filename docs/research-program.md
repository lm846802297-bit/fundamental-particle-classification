# Research Program

## 1. Objective

Determine which particle-statistics structures are possible for fundamental particles in relativistic QFT, which assumptions restrict them to bosons and fermions, and where established classifications leave a precise open problem.

The project treats this as a classification problem with several linked layers. A statistics label alone is not enough to identify a physical particle type.

## 2. Definitions to keep distinct

For each source and argument, specify which object is being discussed:

- **One-particle state:** a physical state singled out by the theory's spectrum and particle interpretation, for example an isolated mass-shell representation when that criterion applies.
- **Superselection sector:** a representation of the observable theory that cannot be connected to another by local observable operations. Record the localization and transportability assumptions used.
- **Field and charge description:** a choice of fields and internal or gauge degrees of freedom used to represent observable sectors.

Do not assume that these notions are interchangeable. A proposed classification must say which one it classifies and how the others enter.

## 3. Comparison axes

For every theorem, model, or proposed particle type, record:

| Axis | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Spacetime | Which dimension, metric, and spacetime symmetry group are assumed? |
| Particle criterion | Is the object an asymptotic particle, a one-particle representation, a localized sector, or something else? |
| Localization | Are charges localized in bounded regions, cones, strings, or by another criterion? |
| Causality | Which locality or commutativity assumptions are imposed on observables and fields? |
| Statistics | Is the exchange structure permutation-based, braid-based, or more general? Which representations or categories occur? |
| Composition | What fusion rules, antiparticles, and sector combinations are allowed? |
| Spin and symmetry | Which spacetime-group representations occur, and what spin-statistics relation is established? |
| Equivalence | Can the description be reformulated through internal or gauge structure without changing the physical sectors? |
| Evidence | Is the claim a theorem, a construction, a conjecture, or a review-level summary? |

## 4. Work packages

### WP0 — Source map

Review primary literature and reliable reviews on:

- spin-statistics theorems and their hypotheses;
- parastatistics and its relation to internal or gauge structure;
- superselection sectors and particle statistics in local QFT;
- braid-group statistics, anyons, and plektons in relativistic theories in low dimensions;
- spacetime-symmetry representations and particle concepts across dimensions;
- results in other dimensions when they introduce distinct assumptions or structures.

For each source, record the exact theorem or construction, definitions, hypotheses, proof strategy, and known limitations. A gap in a review is a lead to investigate, not by itself evidence of novelty.

### WP1 — Classification matrix

Build a dimension-aware matrix using the axes above. Keep separate columns for:

1. spacetime representations;
2. exchange/statistics structures;
3. superselection sectors and localization;
4. fusion and composition;
5. the physical criterion for calling an object a fundamental particle.

### WP2 — Consistency and examples

For each candidate type, identify a relativistic QFT example or a precise obstruction. Check that the example satisfies the claimed locality, spectrum, and particle criteria. Mark statements that rely on idealized assumptions.

### WP3 — Research-gap assessment

Compare the matrix with the primary sources. For each candidate gap, state:

- what is already known;
- the exact unanswered question;
- why existing theorems or constructions do not answer it;
- what mathematical or physical result would close the gap;
- what would distinguish that result from a reformulation or a change of terminology.

### WP4 — Focused result

Select a tractable question only after WP0–WP3. Develop the derivation or construction, verify the assumptions, and submit the result with source history and independent checks.

## 5. Initial acceptance criteria

The initial literature phase is complete when:

- each major classification claim is tied to a primary source;
- theorem assumptions are recorded in a comparable form;
- the matrix distinguishes particle states, sectors, and field descriptions;
- candidate gaps are checked against the cited results;
- unresolved questions are explicitly separated from established conclusions.

These criteria define the first review point. Additional work can be planned after that review without blocking completion of the literature map.
