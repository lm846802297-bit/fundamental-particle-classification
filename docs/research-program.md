# Research Program

## 1. Objective and current logical status

The central question is whether fundamental particles beyond ordinary bosons and fermions exist in relativistic quantum field theory (QFT).

**The broad existence question is already answered affirmatively when 2+1-dimensional spacetime is included.** Massive anyons and non-Abelian plektons occur as particle-bearing sectors in local relativistic quantum theories. Their one-particle states are unitary representations of the 2+1D Poincare group, and a relativistic spin-statistics theorem has been proved for massive braid-statistics sectors under explicit mass-spectrum assumptions.

This established result is the project's positive benchmark. The research program must not present the existence of relativistic anyons as an open conjecture. The remaining objective is to determine whether there are further fundamental-particle classes beyond the known anyon/plekton structures, and to establish the strongest dimension-dependent classification and no-go results.

The main scientific outcomes are therefore:

1. **Further existence:** an explicit or theorem-level demonstration of a fundamental particle class beyond ordinary Bose/Fermi statistics and beyond the already established relativistic anyon/plekton cases.
2. **Dimension-dependent no-go:** a proof that no further class is possible under a precisely stated set of assumptions, identifying which assumptions are essential.
3. **Classification result:** a rigorous map of which particle and statistics structures are allowed in each spacetime dimension and localization regime.

A result that only identifies a new species, unusual mass or charge, higher spin, continuous spin, helicity, or a different field notation does not by itself establish new exchange statistics. Such structures remain relevant because they constrain the available particle representations and the assumptions entering spin-statistics theorems.

## 2. Definitions and particle criteria

For every source and argument, specify which object is being discussed:

- **Physical one-particle state:** a state in the physical Hilbert space with a particle interpretation. In theories where the standard criterion applies, this may be an isolated positive-energy mass-shell representation. If that criterion is unavailable, state the alternative particle criterion explicitly.
- **Asymptotic particle:** a state entering the incoming/outgoing scattering-state construction, when scattering theory is available.
- **Superselection sector:** a representation of the observable theory that cannot be connected to another by local observable operations. Record the localization, transportability, and spectrum assumptions used.
- **Field and charge description:** a choice of fields and internal or gauge degrees of freedom used to represent physical sectors. A field label or gauge-dependent excitation is not automatically a distinct physical particle.

These notions are related but not interchangeable. A proposed candidate must specify which physical object carries the claimed non-Bose/non-Fermi property and how it is represented in the observable theory.

## 3. Established positive benchmark: relativistic anyons and plektons

The following result is part of the baseline:

- In 2+1D, the universal cover of the massive little group permits arbitrary real spin.
- Massive anyon one-particle states can be constructed as unitary ray representations of the 2+1D Poincare group.
- Their field localization is string-like; the corresponding charged sectors are described using spacelike-cone localization in the algebraic formulation. These are distinct but related localization descriptions, and neither should be conflated with point-local or bounded-region localization.
- Abelian braid-group representations give anyons; non-Abelian braid-group representations give plektons. The relevant braid statistics are tied to the noncompact localization regime; the spacetime dimension alone is not sufficient.
- For massive braid-statistics sectors in 2+1D, the spin-statistics relation is
  \[
  e^{2\pi i s}=\omega,
  \]
  where s is the particle spin and omega is the statistics phase in the Abelian case. For non-Abelian sectors, the exchange data are matrix-valued.
- Mund's theorem derives the relation from local relativistic quantum theory under a mass-gap assumption and a finite-degeneracy condition for the relevant particle mass.

These are fundamental relativistic particle sectors, not condensed-matter quasiparticles. The absence of point-local charged fields does not disqualify the physical particle state; the localization criterion must be stated explicitly.

Primary sources:
- R. Jackiw and V. P. Nair, “Relativistic wave equation for anyons,” Phys. Rev. D 43, 1933 (1991), DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevD.43.1933.
- J. Mund, “Modular Localization of Massive Particles with ‘Any’ Spin in d=2+1,” arXiv:hep-th/0208195.
- J. Mund, “The Spin-Statistics Theorem for Anyons and Plektons in d=2+1,” Commun. Math. Phys. 286, 1159–1180 (2009), DOI: 10.1007/s00220-008-0628-9, arXiv:0801.3621.
- K. Fredenhagen, K.-H. Rehren, and B. Schroer, “Superselection Sectors with Braid Group Statistics and Exchange Algebras. I. General Theory,” Commun. Math. Phys. 125, 201–226 (1989), DOI: 10.1007/BF01217906.

## 4. Definitions and equivalence tests for further candidates

For every proposed candidate, answer:

1. **Fundamental status:** Is it a particle of the underlying relativistic QFT, rather than a composite bound state, medium-dependent quasiparticle, collective excitation, or emergent excitation?
2. **Physical state:** What is its physical one-particle state or particle-bearing superselection sector?
3. **Beyond known classes:** Which invariant distinguishes it from ordinary Bose/Fermi particles and from the already established relativistic anyon/plekton structures?
4. **Observable distinction:** Does the distinction survive passage to the physical observable algebra and physical Hilbert space?
5. **Inequivalence:** Can the candidate be rewritten as ordinary bosons or fermions with additional internal/gauge degrees of freedom without changing physical predictions? If yes, it is not a new statistics class for this project.
6. **Consistency:** Does the construction satisfy the stated spectrum, positivity, covariance, locality/causality, and interaction requirements?

A mathematical exchange algebra alone is insufficient. It must define a physically admissible fundamental particle in a relativistic QFT.

## 6. Work packages

### WP0 — Primary-source map

Review primary literature and reliable reviews on:

- Wigner's classification and irreducible representations of the Poincare group, including massive and massless little groups across spacetime dimensions;
- higher-spin and continuous-spin representations, while distinguishing new spacetime representations from new statistics;
- spin-statistics theorems, their hypotheses, and dimension dependence;
- parastatistics and its relation to internal, gauge, and superselection structures;
- braid-group statistics, abelian anyons, non-Abelian plektons, and relativistic QFT in 2+1 dimensions;
- infinite statistics and other generalized exchange or field algebras;
- superselection sectors, DHR theory, Doplicher-Roberts reconstruction, and related extensions;
- point-, string-, and cone-localized fields, observable locality, and causal localization;
- spectrum conditions, positivity, unitarity, CPT, and interaction constraints relevant to candidate theories.

For each source, record the exact theorem or construction, definitions, hypotheses, proof strategy, physical state space, and known limitations. A gap in a review is a lead to investigate, not evidence of novelty.

### WP1 — Dimension-aware candidate matrix

Build a matrix for 1+1, 2+1, 3+1, and higher-dimensional spacetime. Keep separate columns for:

1. physical particle criterion;
2. Poincare representations;
3. exchange/statistics structures;
4. localization and causality;
5. superselection sectors and fusion;
6. physical inequivalence to ordinary Bose/Fermi and known anyon/plekton descriptions;
7. consistency status and primary-source evidence.

Do not infer that a candidate exists merely because a mathematical representation or exchange algebra can be written down.

### WP2 — Candidate verification

For each candidate type, identify an explicit relativistic QFT construction or a precise obstruction. Verify that:

- the particle is fundamental under the project's definition;
- the physical Hilbert space and observable sector are well defined;
- the candidate's non-Bose/Fermi property is not a gauge or internal-label reformulation;
- the construction meets its claimed dimension, spectrum, locality, and unitarity assumptions;
- interactions, antiparticles, and multiparticle composition are treated consistently where required.

### WP3 — Dimension-dependent no-go assessment

Compare verified candidates with the relevant primary theorems. In particular:

- In 3+1D, establish precisely how bounded-region DHR localization, finite statistics, permutation symmetry, and Doplicher-Roberts reconstruction constrain possible sectors.
- For massive charges localized in spacelike cones, determine what the Buchholz-Fredenhagen framework proves and which statistics remain possible.
- In 1+1D, distinguish braid-like statistics of chiral/conformal sectors from the existence of massive asymptotic fundamental particles.
- In dimensions above 3+1, analyze the point-particle configuration-space topology and the higher-dimensional little groups, then establish which conclusions follow from topology alone and which require a QFT theorem.

For each obstruction, state the theorem's hypotheses and whether relaxing any one of them admits a genuine candidate.

### WP4 — Focused result

Select a tractable question only after WP0–WP3. Develop the construction or proof, verify its assumptions, and submit the result with source history and independent checks.

## 7. Initial literature-phase acceptance criteria

The initial literature phase is complete when:

- the established 2+1D anyon/plekton existence result is documented from primary sources;
- the principal Wigner/Poincare representations and spin-statistics theorem assumptions are recorded;
- theorem assumptions are recorded in a comparable form;
- the matrix distinguishes particle states, asymptotic particles, sectors, and field descriptions;
- candidate statistics are tested for physical inequivalence to ordinary Bose/Fermi theories and to the known anyon/plekton benchmark;
- dimension, Poincare representation, localization, and consistency conditions are represented;
- candidate gaps are checked against the cited results;
- unresolved questions are separated from established conclusions.

These criteria close the literature-baseline phase. Further model construction or no-go work belongs to the next phase and does not block completion of the baseline.
